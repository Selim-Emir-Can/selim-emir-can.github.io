0. Changes from v2 and from the original study, and why

1. Questions and hypotheses

  1. Primary. When two stained-looking images of the same field are shown side by side (no input), can independent expert readers identify the chemically cleared-and-stained (C&SF) reference better than chance for ClariDi outputs on held-out regions under the spatial split v2 (the setting of the manuscript's proof-of-concept claim)? H0: P(correct) = 0.5; H1: P(correct) != 0.5 (two-sided).
  2. Key secondary. The same for ClariDi leave-one-specimen-out (LOSO) outputs, and the LOSO - spatial difference.
  3. Secondary. Single-image source judgment, plausibility (3 items), tissue identification, inter-reader agreement, learning effect.

Decision rule for the primary endpoint (fixed in advance, three outcomes): (a) p < 0.05 and estimate > 0.5: readers can discriminate; (b) p < 0.05 and estimate < 0.5: readers systematically prefer the virtual image (reported as such, not as indistinguishability); (c) otherwise, "not distinguishable within the margin" only if the 90% CI lies inside [0.40, 0.60] (TOST, alpha 0.05 per side); if not, inconclusive. Non-significance alone is never reported as equivalence.

Equivalence margin justification. In 2AFC, P(correct) = 0.5 + d/2 where d is the share of pairs in which the reader truly detects the virtual image and guesses otherwise; 0.60 corresponds to true detection in at most 20% of pairs (d' = sqrt(2) z(0.60) = 0.36). A narrower margin (0.45-0.55) would need roughly four times the reader-tile information (on the order of 40-50 readers at the simulated variances) and is not feasible; the margin was fixed before any data and is not changed.

2. Stimuli (output-blind, fixed seed)

  • Population: the 753 tiles (11 pieces, one mouse); every tile has held-out predictions under both protocols.
  • Eligibility: not masked; UA tissue fraction >= 0.75 (share of UA pixels with max(R,G,B) > 13); no registration padding in the reference (above). 506 of 601 tissue-eligible tiles remain.
  • Selection: 180 tiles, no two sharing pixels: brain A, E, G, H, I 16 each and D 10 (D has only 19 eligible tiles) = 90; heart B, C, F, J, K 18 each = 90. Within a piece one 5x5 tile is drawn first (a 5x5 tile covers about four 10x10 tiles; a proportional 5x5 share would exhaust the small pieces), overlapping 10x10 tiles are removed, the rest is drawn from 10x10 tiles. numpy.random.default_rng(20261020), pieces A..K, pools sorted by tile id. Block assignment within tissue: tiles ordered by piece (random within piece), every third to Block 2: Block 1 (2AFC) 120 tiles (60 brain, 60 heart); Block 2 (single image) 60 tiles (30, 30), 15 of them anchors (8 brain, 7 heart; 5 per source).
  • Images per tile: C&SF reference; ClariDi spatial-v2 output (spatial_v2/sp_primary) and LOSO output (claridi_primary), both draw 0 (gen_idx 0, seed 1234), the prespecified single generation; no draw or tile is chosen by appearance. Outputs were read only after the tile set was fixed, to build the packets and the format QC.
  • Catch stimuli: references of eligible, unselected tiles sharing no pixels with study tiles: 6 Block-1 pairs (intact vs block-shuffled, 8 x 8 grid of 32-px blocks permuted, same tissue) and 2 Block-2 block-shuffled images. Never analysed as data.
  • Presentation: 256 x 256 PNG shown at 384 x 384 CSS px with the browser's default smooth interpolation, identical for all sources; processing as in §0 (6). Format QC (keys/stimulus_qc.csv): all images 8-bit RGB 256 x 256, identical byte size, blue = 0. Content differences that remain are part of what is evaluated (e.g. 2 of 202 spatial outputs show a black edge region produced by the model; outputs are never edited or excluded by appearance).

3. Readers, eligibility, blinding

  • Target 12 independent readers (minimum 9) covering neuroanatomy, cardiac histology and fluorescence microscopy (at least two per area where possible). Recorded in the form: areas, position, years of experience, frequency of fluorescence image reading, familiarity with clearing and with virtual staining / generative models, device, screen/window size, pixel ratio.
  • Attestation in the form (yes/no, recorded, never forced): not an author; not involved in model development, output generation or image selection; has not seen the manuscript/preprint, its figures or the ClariDi web pages; has not taken part in the pilot; will work alone without image-analysis software, zoom tools or opening the image files.
  • Reader IDs R01-R12 are handed out in random order (shuffle recorded with date); ID determines the sequences (Block 1: i mod 2, Block 2: i mod 3), so the assignment of persons to sequences is random (basis of the Block-2 randomization test).
  • Readers are told that the set contains C&SF references and virtual outputs, not the proportions, model names, protocols or that two models exist. No feedback is given at any time. Key files never leave the coordinator.
  • Residual risks stated up front: pixel-identical images are shared across readers under different names (mitigated by the instruction not to discuss images); a determined reader could analyse pixel statistics with software (forbidden by instruction and attestation).

4. Design and procedure

  • Offline HTML form (one per reader, own images/ folder); works from a local folder or a web server; progress saved in browser localStorage after every page; no back navigation; Part 1 locked when Part 2 starts; images hidden until both have loaded, and Next is enabled only after loading and answering. Exports CSV and JSON (reader ID, build id, attestations, expertise, display, start/finish timestamps, per-trial response time, choice time, tab-hidden time, displayed image width, load errors). Estimated 50-60 min, can be split.
  • Block 2 first (62 pages: 60 study images + 2 catch; one image per page, questions underneath): non-anchor source = (rank + i mod 3) mod 3, i.e. 20 per source per reader and each non-anchor tile under every source across sequences; random order; one catch image per half.
  • Block 1 second (126 trials: 120 + 6 catch): protocol by (rank + i mod 2) parity, 60 + 60; reference on side A in exactly 30 of 60 trials per protocol; random order, one catch trial per sixth of the block. Question: "Which image is the C&SF reference?" (A / B, forced) and confidence 1-4. Block 2 precedes Block 1 so that single-image judgments are made before readers have compared any pair.

5. Endpoints and analysis (code/analyze_reader_study_v3.py, frozen)

  • Primary: proportion of Block-1 spatial trials in which the reference is chosen; Obuchowski-Rockette with Hillis denominator df, readers and tiles random, between-reader covariance by jackknife over tiles (split-plot: reader pairs without common tiles contribute zero covariance). Type I error of this exact implementation and design was verified by simulation (§6).
  • Key secondary: the same for LOSO trials; LOSO - spatial difference by logistic GEE with a protocol term (clusters = tiles).
  • Secondary (effect sizes with 95% CI; no equivalence claims from non-significance): Block-2 source judgment: source x response table; prespecified test = randomization test of the chi-square statistic over reader-to-sequence assignments (exact when <= 50,000 assignments, otherwise 20,000 Monte Carlo draws, seed 2026; anchors fixed); supportive NominalGEE; discrimination index P('reference' | reference) - P('reference' | virtual) with reader x tile bootstrap CI. Plausibility Q1-Q3 (Low = 1, Medium = 2, High = 3): OrdinalGEE (rating ~ source + tissue + reader, clusters = tiles), cumulative odds ratios virtual vs reference, Holm across the three items within each comparison. Tissue identification per source (never pooled). Agreement on anchors: Fleiss' kappa (source, tissue), Krippendorff's alpha ordinal (Q1-Q3). Learning: accuracy vs trial position (GEE slope) and first vs second half, per protocol. Accuracy by confidence (descriptive).
  • Sensitivity: reader x tile cluster bootstrap CI; trials with rt >= 1 s; trials without load error or tab switch; reader-level accuracy; exact binomial (descriptive, ignores clustering).

Decision: removing the repetition with the old 60 tiles would cost about 15-25 points of power; 120 tiles restore and slightly exceed v2. Target 12 readers (two full replicates of the 2 x 3 sequence design; power 0.96 at P(correct) = 0.62 and 0.89 probability of demonstrating equivalence if readers truly guess); minimum 9 (0.88 and 0.74). With fewer than 9 analysable readers the analysis is run and reported, but the equivalence outcome is described as underpowered. Simulated type I error is 0.05-0.06 (Monte Carlo SE 0.005), i.e. the OR-Hillis test can be slightly liberal with 6-9 readers; this is reported. A tile-only GEE ignores reader variance and is supportive only.

7. Exclusions, missing data, deviations (prespecified, applied mechanically by the script)

  • Reader excluded from all analyses if: < 5 of 6 Block-1 catch trials correct; any eligibility attestation failed (author / involved / seen / pilot participant = yes, or works alone = no); < 80% of study trials answered; > 20% of answered Block-1 trials faster than 1 s. Reader excluded from Block-2 analyses only if both Block-2 catch images were rated "High" for architecture coherence. Exclusions are reported with reasons; replacement readers receive the next unused IDs (R13...), built with the same code, before any analysis.
  • No trial-level exclusion in the primary analysis (rt < 1 s and tab switches only in sensitivity analyses). Unanswered trials are omitted (complete case, no imputation). No tile is removed after outputs or responses have been seen.
  • Response files must carry the main build id; pilot files and files from other builds are refused by the script.
  • Any deviation (e.g. fewer readers, a reader using a phone, a form re-issued after a technical failure) is reported as a deviation with its date.

8. Code, pilot and data handling

  • Analysis code frozen and tested only on SIMULATED data (SIMULATED_test/, all files prefixed SIMULATED, generated by code/simulate_reader_data_v3.py with known parameters, which the analysis recovers) and on dummy exports produced by headless (jsdom) click-throughs of the forms. No result exists yet.
  • Pilot: a public pilot (reader_study_pilot/ on the ClariDi site, unlisted, noindex) uses a separate set of 36 + 5 tiles not in the study, two forms (PILOT-A/B, 13 + 26 items), and carries its own build id. Pilot answers are used only to check clarity, display and timing; they are never analysed with study data, and pilot participants cannot be study readers. Changes made after the pilot (wording, layout) are listed with dates before the deposit; if the deposit precedes the pilot, post-pilot changes are deposited as an amendment before the first study reader starts.

9. Limitations stated in advance

One animal (11 pieces); single 256 x 256 tiles of 2D projections; references registered with 15-50 um error, so the 2AFC cannot evaluate nucleus-level correspondence and was designed to avoid alignment cues rather than to measure correspondence; readers judge realism, not diagnostic utility; the virtual images are one draw of a stochastic model; reader expertise is heterogeneous and self-reported.
