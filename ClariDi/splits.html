How the split works. Each sample's tiles are grouped into spatial units (a 5×5 crop together with the 10×10 crops inside it, so the two grids never put overlapping pixels in different partitions). Units are ordered along the sample's long axis and cut into 5 bands. In fold k, band k is the test set, the neighbouring band (k+1; band 3 for fold 4) is val, and the other three bands are train. Every tile is tested exactly once across the 5 folds, and every specimen appears in every partition. Exception: D part 0 collapsed into 2 units and has empty partitions in several folds (fixed in split v2, which all current results use). This split tests within-specimen interpolation, not generalization to new specimens. A test band can share a tile edge (no pixels) with a training band. Hover a tile for its id, unit and role in every fold; click it to open it in the tile viewer. Hatched = no tile at this grid. Keys: 0–4 fold, a all folds, b bands, s grid, c image, u units.
